SUMMARY
Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SPCX -9.14%) reported a period of significant scaling as management transitioned the company toward a vertically integrated infrastructure provider across launch, connectivity, and super intelligence. The company achieved a record 90% share of global mass to orbit while simultaneously accelerating the deployment of its terrestrial compute clusters to reach 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate capacity. Management stated that the combination of launch leadership, recurring Starlink revenue, and the expansion of Super Intelligence infrastructure is driving meaningful operating leverage. The company is aggressively investing in Starship development and next-generation satellite constellations, with fourth-quarter capital expenditures projected to nearly double sequentially to support the accelerated build path of its compute centers.
Management announced the development of the Louisiana spaceport, with Musk stating the facility will eventually include "on the order of 20 or more launch towers" to enable millions of tonnes to orbit annually.
The company expects Starship to reach a weekly launch cadence in the first half of 2027, accelerating to twice weekly or higher by the end of that year as rapid reusability is finalized.
Musk indicated that the company prefers short-term compute rental contracts at spot market rates, stating, "our belief is that the value of compute per watt will increase over time, not decrease."
SpaceX is leveraging its proprietary rocket engine casting technology to manufacture terrestrial turbine components, which Musk claimed allows the company to bring power online 12 to 18 months faster than competitors.
Management detailed a multi-year semiconductor road map, aiming for a test chip in mid-2027 and volume production at the Terafab facility by the third quarter of 2028.
Gwynne Shotwell reported that aviation production rates have quadrupled since the beginning of the year, with a goal to double that rate again before the end of the year to meet aircraft demand.
The company entered into an agreement to acquire 800 megahertz low-band spectrum, which management stated will pave the way for Starlink Mobile to become a major mobile carrier in the United States.
INDUSTRY GLOSSARY
Starlink V3: The third-generation satellite in the Starlink constellation, delivering significantly higher bandwidth capacity per launch compared to previous versions.
Colossus II: The company's massive supercomputing cluster located in Memphis, utilized for training and inference of super intelligence models.
Terafab: The planned high-volume semiconductor fabrication facility designed to produce super intelligence chips.
Grok Bot: An agentic super intelligence assistant designed for both consumer use and enterprise workflows.
GB300: High-performance graphics processing units used in the buildout of super intelligence compute infrastructure.
Starfall: A space capsule designed to provide routine, low-cost access to microgravity environments for scientific and medical research.
Starmind: A next-generation satellite constellation focused on high-bandwidth super intelligence connectivity.
Nameplate Compute: The total theoretical power capacity of a data center or supercomputing cluster, measured in gigawatts.
